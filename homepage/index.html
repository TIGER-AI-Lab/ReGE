Abstract

How can an agent turn its own attempts into better execution?

Repeated attempts at interactive tasks uncover facts, useful operations, and failures that can inform subsequent execution. We introduce ReGE, a framework for turning this recorded experience into guidance for a fresh attempt. Its main implementation, ReGE-Syn, uses a single coach call to jointly synthesize bounded execution records and available artifacts into self-contained guidance, connecting task-specific findings with proposed actions and relevant pitfalls. A student starts from the task's initial environment state and adapts this guidance to current observations. Source generation, synthesis, and student execution use the same base model, without parameter updates or a stronger teacher. Across four benchmarks, ReGE improves accuracy by an average of 9.9 percentage points for GPT and 8.8 percentage points for Claude compared with unguided execution. On matched BrowseComp-Plus subsets, ReGE-Syn reduces guidance-construction tokens by 98.2% on GPT and 95.5% on Claude relative to adapted AggAgent, with observed success. Combined construction and execution tokens decrease by 15.7% and 35.4%, respectively, counting input plus output and excluding source generation. BrowseComp-Plus ablations further associate access to process information with fewer student steps and show that joint synthesis produces substantially shorter guidance than Direct history or Independent summaries, with observed success. These results demonstrate how agents can use their own recorded experience to improve subsequent task completion, and suggest a low-cost way to consolidate the execution records of parallel agent systems such as agent swarms.

01Method

Record, synthesize, re-execute

ReGE (Re-execution Guided by Experience) needs only the agent's recorded attempts at a task. The guidance is built without correctness labels, and the student re-executes the task in a fresh environment with the same base model.

1Record

Attempts form a source pool

The model attempts the task four times. Each attempt is rendered as text without any LLM summary: reasoning or action narratives and commands in step order, then the final artifacts, such as files, SQL results or a submitted answer. Correctness labels and raw observations are left out, and long fields are truncated.

2Synthesize

One coach call writes the guidance

A coach reads all attempts together and writes a plan in execution order, placing the exact paths, values, commands and formats the attempts uncovered at the steps where they matter, along with the pitfalls they exposed. When the records support a candidate answer or artifact, the plan states it with a way to verify it.

3Re-execute

A fresh student acts on it

The student starts from the task's initial state with the instruction and the guidance. Files and state from the earlier attempts do not carry over, so it adopts, adjusts or skips each proposed operation against what it observes, and the benchmark grades the result.

03Findings

What makes recorded experience useful

Ablations on BrowseComp-Plus with claude-sonnet-5, and a breakdown of all benchmarks by how many source attempts succeeded.

The experience, not the coach
75.7% → 88.3% no attempts vs four

A coach that sees no source attempts (Closed-book) solves 75.7% of 300 queries, close to Control's 74.7%. One recorded attempt lifts success to 84.3%, two to 86.3% and four to 88.3%.

Process records, not just answers
14.6 → 9.1 mean student steps

Synthesizing from final submitted answers only (FAO) solves 85.0% instead of 88.3%. On queries with a single submitted source answer (6.3% of queries), full records solve 94.7% and FAO 47.4%; even when all four sources submit answers, mean student steps fall from 8.9 to 4.8.

Mixed pools gain the most
+13.0 to +48.0 points over Control

When some but not all source attempts succeed, ReGE-Syn gains 13.0–21.6 points on Terminal-Bench 2.0, Spider2-Lite and AndroidWorld, and 48.0 (GPT) and 35.2 (Claude) on BrowseComp-Plus. When every attempt fails, gains are small and inconsistent.

Joint synthesis keeps guidance short
5,063 characters vs 20,571

On matched queries, the joint guide is about a quarter as long as Direct history or Independent summaries, solves 88.3% against 88.3% and 86.9%, and uses 19.8% and 31.8% fewer total tokens. Other forms also work: ReGE-A (explanation and answer) reaches 90.7% at 0.68× the total tokens, though with gpt-5.6-luna on 100 queries it solves 80% versus 82%, and ReGE-Sel reaches 89.3% with 21% fewer student tokens.

04Cases

From recorded experience to a fresh execution

Two claude-sonnet-5 runs from the paper. The student checks the guidance against its own environment and changes course where needed.

Terminal-Bench 2.0db-wal-recovery

Recovering a database from its log

Recorded experience

Successful attempts identify the XOR decoding key 0x42 for the database's write-ahead log. Another attempt opens main.db before making a backup and loses main.db-wal, leaving the recovery incomplete.

Synthesized guidance

The guide keeps the decoding key and tells the student to back up both files before opening the database, decode and check the log, and then recover the records.

Student verification and adjustment

The student backs up its files, verifies the key and the decoded log, and moves the main.db-shm cleanup, which the guide offered only as a fallback, ahead of replacing the log and opening the database. It exports 11 ordered records to recovered.json and passes all seven official tests.

AndroidWorldBrowserMultiply

Completing a browser task from unsuccessful attempts

Recorded experience

Repeated taps on task.html leave the file manager unchanged. Two attempts discover the route long-press → More options → Open with → Chrome, but all four exhaust their 22-step budgets before completing the task.

Synthesized guidance

The guide keeps this opening sequence, warns against repeated single taps and describes Chrome's setup prompts. It asks the student to read the five numbers from the page and submit their product.

Student verification and adjustment

The student uses the opening sequence immediately. When dismissing Chrome's sync prompt returns it to the file manager, it repeats Open with → Chrome, records 9, 10, 9, 5 and 5 during the five clicks and enters 20,250. The page shows "Success!" after 19 steps, within the same 22-step budget.
